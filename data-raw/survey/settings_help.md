**Swath filter.** The survey database has damage polygons, not one swath per tornado. For a paired tornado, the polygons that cross the surveyed centerline on the same date (within one day) are kept when they are rated EF0 to EF5 and at least 90% of their area lies within a distance from the centerline equal to the SPC path width plus 500 m (this removes polygons of neighboring tornadoes and straight-line wind). The kept polygons are merged into the swath, which is usable when it lies within 200 m of at least 50% of the centerline's length. None of the four values (90%, 500 m, 200 m, 50%) comes from an external source. Each was changed in turn (table in `data-raw/known_issues.md`). The number of tornadoes with a usable swath is 2,941 with the values used and ranges from 2,909 to 2,952 across the alternatives; the largest loss is 32 tornadoes (500 m -> 250 m) and the largest gain 11 (500 m -> 1,000 m). For tornadoes with a usable swath under both settings the swath area does not change.

**Polygon resolution.** The damage polygons were fetched with the service's generalization set to 0.0002 degrees (about 20 m), and the polygons stored in `survey_tracks` for plotting and distances are simplified to 50 m (no outline point moves by more than 50 m). Against full-resolution polygons, the area of the 20 m swaths has a median change of -0.18% and a 90th percentile of the absolute change of 13.64% (largest 126.8%); no tornado changes usability. The 50 m polygons differ from the 20 m swaths by a median of -0.37% and a 90th percentile of 20.37% (largest 322.2%), which matters for small swaths. The exposure columns of `survey_exposure` come from the 20 m swaths, so only plots and `centroid_dist_km` use the simplified polygons.
